ICLR 2027Under review

Do We Need Dependency-Ready Decoding for Masked Diffusion Language Models?

L. Berrayana, C. Malon, Y. Ishibashi, M. Oyamada

PDF

Masked diffusion LMs can decode tokens in any order, which means a token can be revealed before the tokens it depends on. Framing decoding as graph path-finding, we show that these dependency violations hurt accuracy on Sudoku and GSM8K. We introduce a KL-based violation measure μ and prove that a max-margin rule with margin above √(2μ) keeps the top-1 prediction stable when any single masked token is revealed, with zero top-1 flips across 17,885 qualifying Sudoku reveals.

Latent-DARM: Bridging Discrete Diffusion and Autoregressive Models for Reasoning

L. Berrayana*, A. Heakl*, M. A. Sohail, T. Hofmann, S. Khan, W. Chen

Paper Microsoft Research Earlier version

A discrete diffusion model plans, an autoregressive model writes the answer, and the two communicate through a learned projector in latent space instead of text. Compared with text-based interfaces, accuracy rises from 27.0% to 36.0% on DART-5 and from 0.0% to 14.0% on AIME 2024, approaching state-of-the-art reasoning models with under 2.2% of their token budget.

ACL 2025GEM² Workshop

Are Bias Evaluation Methods Biased?

L. Berrayana, S. Rooney, L. Garcés-Erice, I. Giurgiu

Paper arXiv

We rank a set of representative LLMs for social bias using three widely used evaluation methods and compare the resulting rankings. The methods disagree, so we close with recommendations on how the community should use such benchmarks.
